KB GPT Epistemic Roles: Design Rationale

The Design Challenge

Knowledge Building requires students to treat ideas as improvable objects by identifying gaps in their explanations, pushing from observation to mechanism, evaluating which ideas are worth pursuing, and working toward higher-order explanations through collaborative discourse. These are precisely the epistemic moves that a capable language model can perform quickly and fluently on students' behalf.

The risk is epistemic substitution: students receive a well-formed response from KB GPT and treat it as the output of their inquiry, rather than as input to further thinking.

The design challenge is therefore not how to maximize KB GPT's usefulness, but how to make it helpful in ways that preserve and deepen students' epistemic agency. This requires strategically delineating the roles KB GPT plays by specifying what it maps, questions, or describes, while also making explicit what students must decide, evaluate, and produce in response. The four epistemic roles below are hence designed to prevent cognitive offloading to AI by ensuring that the most consequential thinking always remains with the students.

Framing

KB GPT is a partner in the shared mission of idea improvement. Partnership means the same goal and co-production of ideas between AI and students.

The Stocktaker Coherence Type Making ideas more coherent Group has generated many notes on KF but cannot articulate a clear main claim; keeps going in circles without a clear direction. [Import All Notes]"Take stock — What are our key ideas, what evidence backs them, what are we assuming without evidence, and what is missing?" Lists an inventory of ideas and evidence without judging whether they are good or bad. Review the Stocktaker's inventory. Decide what you need more or less of. KB GPT takes stock of what's there. You choose what to do about it.
The Outsider Generative Type Making ideas more generative Group has been working on ideas for a while and has lost the ability to evaluate them freshly; struggling to decide which direction is worth pursuing. [Import All Notes / Select Notes]"You have no stake in any of these ideas. From the outside, which seems interesting to develop further, and why?" Reads all ideas without attachment. Identifies what seems most generative or underexplored — without declaring any idea correct. Consider the Outsider's view, then make your own judgment call. Justify your choice to your group.
The Digger Explanatory Type Making ideas more explanatory Group has observations from fieldwork but explanation stays at the surface — they can describe what they noticed but not why it happens or how things work. [Select Notes]"Keep asking us why and how until we cannot answer anymore. Do not fill in the gaps — just keep digging until our explanation runs out." Keeps asking the next question deeper, from observation and description to rationale and mechanism, from 'what' to 'how' and 'why'. Answer each question the Digger asks. Find out exactly what your explanation lacks. That is where your real inquiry begins.
The Mixer Integrative Type Making ideas come together Group wants to explore the relationship between two or more ideas — from their own notes, other groups' KF posts, or the nature expert — and senses a connection worth examining. [Select Notes]"What is the relationship between these notes? Where do they agree, differ, or complicate each other? Do not tell us what to conclude." Brings ideas side by side and describes the relationship honestly — agreement, difference, contradiction, or something unexpected — without resolving it. Consider the relationship between ideas the Mixer revealed. Decide what this means for what you are discussing.
